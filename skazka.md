## the rules

### 01. One guide, the whole trip.

The same person every day, from the first airport door to the last goodbye.
You will know who it is before you arrive.

### 02. Small parties only.

Two, four or six of you. One vehicle, one guide, one chef.
We do not pool strangers. Ever.

### 03. The itinerary is a hypothesis.

Not a contract. If the leopard cubs come back, we stay another night.

### 04. Route around the storm.

If a storm cuts a road, we find another way instead of cancelling a day.
Redundancy on vehicles, power and water.

### 05. Explain the land.

How the Maasai built their enkaji to breathe.
How the railway laid in 1899 redrew the migrations you will see.

### 06. Leave room to do nothing.

Rest days are built in. Many trips finish in Lamu with three days of doing very little.

### 07. Every enquiry, read personally.

No sales team between us. A reply within two working days.

## things we don't do

- sell fixed packages
- hand you between a dozen vendors
- put your party in a vehicle with strangers
- cancel a day because a road washed out
- send a plan without a price and a reason for every choice

## a day in the field

There is no typical day. But most follow the light (East Africa Time, UTC+3):

- 05:30 — tea, brought to the tent before first light
- 06:00 — out for the dawn light
- 10:00 — breakfast in the bush
- 12:00 — rest at camp through the midday heat
- 15:30 — out again, until sunset
- ~18:40 — sunset; near the equator it comes at almost the same time all year
- 20:00 — dinner under the stars, and tomorrow's plan
